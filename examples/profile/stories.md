## A launch I'm proud of

The one-page checkout at Larkfield. Support was nervous because every merchant would see the change at once, so we rolled it out in stages over 6 weeks and gave support a short guide with screenshots and the 10 questions merchants asked most in the first week. Checkout support tickets dropped 22% within 3 months of the rollout (that's support's number, not mine). The staged rollout was the part I'd copy again: it caught a bug in the Canadian address field at 10% of traffic instead of 100%.

## A hard trade-off

Early 2024 we had 5 engineers and room for one big thing. Sales wanted Apple Pay and other digital wallets because 3 large merchants were asking for them. The funnel data said the three-step checkout was the bigger problem. I chose wallets first because it was about 6 weeks of work and it would show merchants we were listening, then started the one-page work right after. I told the sales lead plainly that one-page was second and why, and showed her the funnel chart. She stopped asking in every meeting after that.

## A time I changed someone's mind

At Wrenfield the clinic operations director didn't want a "skip for now" option on the insurance step because front desks needed the insurance details. I showed her that 41% of patients were quitting at that step, then ran a 2-week test at 2 clinics where patients could skip and the front desk collected the card at check-in. Bookings went up and the front desks said it was no extra work. She asked for the same pattern on the new-patient form.

## A metric I moved

Newsletter subscribers at Penhallow went from 410,000 to 640,000 in 2019. I tested 14 signup placements with 4 engineers and 2 designers. The end-of-article box beat everything else. The exit-intent popup did worst and annoyed readers, so we dropped it.

## A failure and what I learned

In 2025 we tested an order bump (a small add-on offered right before payment). It lowered checkout conversion by 3.1% in the first 2 days. I stopped it on day 3, not day 7 as planned, but we still lost a few days of sales for the test merchants. Now every test has a stop-loss agreed up front: pause if conversion drops more than 2% for 2 days in a row.
