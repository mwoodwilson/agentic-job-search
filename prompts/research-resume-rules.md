Research current resume best practices (from the last 2 years) for **{target roles}** at the **{level}** level in
**{field/industry}**, as judged by the hiring managers and recruiters who screen for these roles.

Prioritize, in order: hiring guides published by companies that hire for these roles; recruiter and hiring-manager
surveys and interviews; applicant-tracking-system vendors' own documentation on how resumes are parsed; experienced
career coaches who specialize in this field. Skip generic advice that applies to every resume (typos, contact info).

Answer:
1. What do hiring managers for these roles look for in the first 30 seconds?
2. Which accomplishments and metrics persuade most at this level, and how should they be phrased?
3. Which words, phrases or formats hurt candidates in this field?
4. How should the professional summary be framed for these roles?
5. Which conventions are specific to this field (titles, tools, certifications, portfolio or publication links)?
6. What formatting breaks applicant tracking systems used by employers in this field?

These rules are fixed and must not be contradicted: one page; professional summary of 300–350 characters; every
bullet 200 characters or less; every bullet states a number and its impact; at least 2 time-bound results in each of
the two most recent roles; every claim is true and traceable to the candidate's own notes. Do not propose rules that
loosen or conflict with these.

Output 10–20 rules. For each: the rule in one imperative sentence (25 words or fewer); why it matters (one sentence);
the source(s); evidence strength ("strong evidence" or "common advice"); whether code could check it or it needs
judgment; and a one-line before/after example.
